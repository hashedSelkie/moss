FEMALE SUN JUMPING SPIDER
(Heliophanus dampfi)

Seen on the Moss in June 2022 by a keen local arachnophile, Craig McEwan, this spider is only 5mm in length, recognisable by its yellow-green pedipalps which stand out against its black body. Habitat is raised bogs. More information in our NEWSLETTER SUMMER 2022 (ABOUT US page)

Dragonflies

In July 2011 the Friends of Lenzie Moss welcomed Jonathan Willet to give us a talk and guided walk on dragonflies. Jonathan grew up in Lenzie, and has long had an interest in dragonflies from his days at Lenzie Academy. He has worked for many years variously as a naturalist, ecologist, countryside ranger and biodiversity officer, and is a longstanding member of the British Dragonfly Society. These are some of his photographs.

COMMON HAWKER DRAGONFLY
(aeshna juncia)

Among the largest and fastest flying of the UK dragonflies. IT preys on flies, midges, mosquitoes dragonflies, moths, wasps, and beetles. It catches its prey mid-air, and can hover and fly backwards. Found by acidic pools and boggy damp areas.

GOLDEN RINGED DRAGONFLY
(cordulergaster boltonii)

Another fast, agile and powerful flyer, also found near acidic waters. Its prey consists of small insects like other dragonflies, damselflies, and moths.

Damselflies

photo courtesy of Jackie Gillespie

COMMON BLUE DAMSELFLY
(enallagma cyathigerum)

Damselflies are similar to dragonflies but are usually smaller and more delicate. In flight they tend to flit, and are less agile than dragonflies. At rest, their wings are held against their body, unlike dragonflies where the wings are outstretched at rest. Found near fresh water. They eat other flying insects such as mosquitoes, midges, and flies.

Butterflies

The following butterflies were observed on the Moss during guided walks given by Anthony McCluskey of Butterfly Conservation Scotland in May and June 2022. See NEWSLETTER SUMMER 2022 for his report.

photo courtesy of Jackie Gillespie

GREEN HAIRSTREAK BUTTERFLY
(Callophrys Rubi)

A small butterfly with irridiscent green wings on its underside. It can be seen in spring on calm days in a sheltered area, where it lays its eggs on the local blaeberry bushes, the food of its emerging caterpillars.

GREEN-VEINED WHITE BUTTERFLY
(Pieris napi)

Found in wooded glades on the Moss. It prefers damp areas.

SMALL HEATH BUTTERFLY
(Coenonympha Pamphilus)

The small heath flies near the ground in a fluttering fashion. It normally lays its eggs singly, sometimes on blades of grass. The adults feed on the nectar from plants such as bramble, ragwort and yarrow.

SMALL TORTOISESHELL BUTTERFLY
(Aglais Urticae)

A medium sized butterfly, the adults feed on nectar and their caterpillars on stinging nettles. It prefers a damp climate as the larvae normally grow on drenched leaves.

Moths

In 2004, the Friends of Lenzie Moss purchased a moth trap – a purpose built box containing a light source to attract moths into it, where the moths could be counted and identified, and then released unharmed. The trap was placed in a garden adjacent to the Moss and recorded a total of 94 species of moths, including the Nationally Scarce B Silvery Arches. The full list of moths is available on request.

photo by Martin Harvey courtesy Butterfly Conservation

SILVERY ARCHES MOTH
(polia hepatica)

A decreasing species confined to acid soils in the British Isles, its larva feeding on birch, heather and bog-myrtle.

During Anthony McCluskey’s butterfly walks in 2022 the following day-time moths were also observed.

EULIA MINISTRANA MOTH

A small but beautiful moth, whose golden scales shine even on a dull day. The caterpillars of this species feed on a variety of deciduous trees, so it’s likely to be relying on the trees around the Moss.

SILVER –GROUND CARPET MOTH
(xanthorhoe montanata)

A common moth, with wings of varied colouring on a white background. Prefers damp places with tall vegetation including bogs, woodland and grassland.

ANCYLIS MYRTILLANA MOTH

Found on damp heathland and moss, feeding on blaeberry bushes found on the Moss.

COMMON HEATH MOTH
(ematurga atomaria)

A common moth, found in the Moss’s grassland and wooded areas. Feeds on heather, cross-leaved heath and grassland flowers such as vetch, birds foot trefoil and clover.

Moth Caterpillars

DRINKER MOTH CATERPILLAR
(philudoria potatoria)

Found in damp grassy places, where they feed on a variety of grasses and reeds. They hibernate during the winter in sheltered spots, pupate in Spring, and emerge as a moth in July. Caterpillars emerge again in spring.
